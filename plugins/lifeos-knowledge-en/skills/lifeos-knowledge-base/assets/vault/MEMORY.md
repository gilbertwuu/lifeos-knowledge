# Knowledge base maintenance

Save knowledge that can help with future learning, judgment, creation, or problem-solving. A useful note explains its claim, evidence, conditions for use, and any substantiated relationships to existing knowledge.

## Folders

- `00_Inbox/` holds material worth keeping whose destination is unclear.
- `01_Sources/` holds source material, excerpts, and clearly labeled interpretation.
- `02_Notes/` holds ideas, questions, and tentative conclusions.
- `03_Wiki/` holds reusable knowledge. Start with methods, AI tools, product judgment, content creation, and collaboration preferences. Use the primary purpose of a note to choose a category and keep the index aligned.
- `03_Wiki/Collaboration/` holds the user's confirmed preferences. Keep inferred preferences as candidates for confirmation. Omit collaboration preferences from automatic Wiki relationship updates.

Keep run logs, automation checkpoints, and temporary files with the work that produced them. Keep source claims, user judgments, AI interpretations, and open questions distinct.

## Capture workflow

1. **Screen for lasting value.** Consider future reuse, decision impact, creative value, or a repeatable method. Skip routine chat, one-time answers, status updates, and rephrasing with no new information.
2. **Search and deduplicate.** Start with `INDEX.md`. Read the closest notes and compare claims, evidence, mechanisms, and scope. Skip a true duplicate, extend an existing entry when you have new evidence or a new use case, and create a Wiki note for an independently reusable insight. Preserve conflicting evidence and conditions. Use Sources, Notes, or Inbox for material that is still developing.
3. **Write a useful note.** Explain what the claim says, why it is supported, when it applies, and where it breaks down. Point to the relevant source passage or conversation. Label hypothetical examples and uncertainties. Limit the conclusion when the evidence is limited.
4. **Check relationships.** Read related notes before linking. Document the relationship and its evidence, such as support, application, sequence, limitation, or correction. Use links that resolve, for example `[[03_Wiki/Methods/YYYY-MM-DD_topic|Display title]]`. Obsidian shows backlinks from outbound links. A note can stand alone when no relationship is supported.
5. **Save and verify.** Use a descriptive filename such as `YYYY-MM-DD_topic.md` for new notes. Check the source, path, links, and attachments. Add new entries to `INDEX.md` and update stale summaries when an existing note changes. In Obsidian, spot-check links and backlinks when available. Report saved locations, relationships, and unresolved gaps.

## Read and maintain

Use the index to find the relevant note, read its body, and follow the source when the conclusion depends on it. Read confirmed collaboration preferences when they are relevant. Follow links as far as the task requires. After real use, improve unclear explanations, missing boundaries, weak sourcing, or incorrect relationships. Preserve what changed and why.

When evidence changes, update the note, index, and affected relationships. Preserve earlier conclusions with their original conditions if useful. Resolve moved links before assuming a note is missing. Ask before merging, deleting, or broadly reorganizing existing material.

## Source-specific handling

- **Conversation.** Read the authorized messages and visible answer text. A title or placeholder does not establish the contents. Record what was accessible.
- **External note.** Inspect the body and relevant supplements before classifying. Keep unresolved access or write failures visible.
- **Project knowledge.** Preserve its origin. Move it into the broader vault when the user requests cross-project retention.
- **Learning notes.** Preserve original answers. Promote insights to the Wiki once they become reusable.

## Minimal note formats

- **Source.** Title, date, type, source location, main claims, relevant excerpts, and interpretation or open questions.
- **Note.** Title, date, provisional status, source, what triggered the idea, current reasoning, and what needs checking.
- **Wiki.** Title, short summary, core explanation and example, applicability and limits, supported links, and source details. Add an optional checked image below the summary. Keep the prose independently useful.
